How it’s made

The two laptop speakers play 18 kHz and 19.5 kHz, and the two mics hear the sound reflected off the hand. For each source we measure the change in path length from the I/Q phase (as LLAP does), and a map learned from webcam-taught sessions (teach.html, with MediaPipe hand joints as ground truth) turns path changes into hand movement (Bravo). This screen smooths that position with a One Euro filter, and since sound only measures movement, it drifts slowly back to the center. The finger shape is fixed.

Keep your hand above the laptop’s front edge, 18–25 cm above the desk (25–45 cm from the webcam), and move it briskly like in teach (about 20 cm a second). Very slow movement (below 5–8 cm a second) is hard for sound to catch. Sound only measures movement, so it doesn’t know absolute position, and when your hand stops it drifts slowly back to the center. Height is barely caught with the laptop speakers alone.
